# Style antipatterns — Deliverable B prose rules

> **Note on examples**: Worked examples in this file reference the author's original use case (consulting, oil & gas, mapping, satellite imagery). Read them as *patterns* and substitute your own companies, products, and vacancies. The rules themselves are domain-agnostic.

Living list of prose patterns the user has caught and rejected in past `/cv-draft` output. Each entry is a one-liner: the pattern to avoid + the rewrite rule. Append as new patterns surface; never silently drop entries.

These rules apply to **every line of `cv.md` output** — SUMMARY, bullets, taglines, EDUCATION, SKILLS. They override the master's own phrasing if the master contains the antipattern (the master can be edited later; the CV must ship clean now).

## Rules

- **No em dashes (`—`) anywhere.** Split into two sentences, or use a comma, semicolon, or parentheses. Applies even when the master bullet uses an em dash — rewrite on the way out. *Reason: the user finds them noisy and over-used; the drafter reaches for `—` to staple clauses together where a period would land harder.*
- **No invented or stretched product / domain categories.** Never bridge a master fact to a vacancy-aligned category by inventing a name the master doesn't use. Example: JewelSuite is "subsurface modelling platform" in master; calling it "3D mapping platform" on a TomTom maps CV is a stretch the specialist audience flags immediately. If the master's category and the vacancy's category don't match exactly, use the master's wording verbatim, or drop the category reference entirely (especially in SUMMARY, where the category is most exposed). Never invent a third category to bridge the two. *Reason: specialist readers catch invented terminology in one pass and discount the whole CV. If the drafter is reaching for a category name that doesn't appear in master, that's a signal to drop the reference, not to guess.*
- **Voice register must match the chosen framing.** When the framing is `[ds]` / `[ai]` / `[eng]` / `[fde]` (technical IC), every bullet must read in implementer voice. The bullet's *opening verb* is the primary signal — a fast recruiter scan tags the bullet's voice from its first word. Under a technical IC framing, bullets opening with any of the following PM-ownership verbs are a binding must-fix, regardless of the content that follows:

  **Forbidden opening verbs under `[ai]` / `[eng]` / `[ds]` / `[fde]` framings:** *Led*, *Owned*, *Drove*, *Coordinated*, *Aligned*, *Translated*, *Rolled out*, *Established*, *Championed*, *Orchestrated*, *Ran* (when object is a workshop / discovery / engagement / program — "Ran discovery workshops" is PM; "Ran experiments" or "Ran benchmarks" is IC and OK), *Managed* (when object is people or accounts), *Advised on* (consultant voice, not implementer — use *Contributed to* / *Worked on* if the underlying work was hands-on).

  Additional PM-ownership constructions to reject: *Owned the X product*, *Translated customer needs into epics*, *Aligned teams on clear requirements*, *Established a team of N*, *Coordinated N teams*, *Drove the X roadmap*, *Led B2B engagements end-to-end*, *Led delivery of X*, *Owned enterprise client relationships*.

  **Preferred opening verbs (implementer voice):** *Built*, *Shipped*, *Deployed*, *Designed*, *Architected*, *Trained*, *Mentored*, *Diagnosed*, *Instrumented*, *Debugged*, *Refactored*, *Migrated*, *Implemented*, *Prototyped*, *Wrote*, *Cut*, *Optimised*, *Automated*, *Contributed*.

  If the master bullet's content underneath is genuinely technical, rewrite the opening verb to implementer voice and keep the content. If the content is genuinely PM-ownership (roadmap ownership, discovery-workshop facilitation, stakeholder alignment) and cannot be honestly rewritten as implementer-work, drop the bullet from the CV — it does not belong under a technical IC framing. The bullet's function tag in master is a soft hint; the bullet's *voice on emitted output* is binding.

  Mirror rule for the reverse direction: under a `[pm]` framing, implementer-voice bullets are fine but a section of pure `Built X` / `Trained X` bullets reads as IC and undermines a PM screen — rebalance with at least one ownership / coordination bullet.

  *Reason: keyword and content match alone don't guarantee voice match. `[pm]`-tagged bullets pulled into a `[ds]` framing made the Baker Hughes section on the TomTom CV read as Product Owner even though the framing said Senior Data Scientist. Iter #27 tightened after the Enjins Senior AI Engineer interview (2026-08-04) closed with "obviously product person" — post-interview transcript analysis surfaced the "Led B2B AI engagements end-to-end (ideation, qualification, and realisation)" bullet under the Rhodaris `[ai][eng]` framing as a voice-register violation the critic missed on loop 1 despite passing every other check. Explicit forbidden-verb enumeration removes the interpretive room the critic was using to rationalize "Led …" as engineer-adjacent.*
- **SUMMARY headline must not letter-mirror the JD title.** The SUMMARY's opening headline (first sentence of the paragraph) carries the CV's identity claim. When the JD title bundles (a) a generic function label ("Senior Technical Product Manager", "Data and AI Specialist") + (b) a scope specifier the JD adds for THIS posting ("Platform & Data Ingestion", "Reporting", "Marketing", "Growth"), the headline emits (a) + a **one-level-up generalization** of (b) — never (b) verbatim. Test: strip the JD title of its scope compound. If the remainder is a defensible headline on its own, use it. If the JD scope IS defensibly the user's genuine tenure focus, keep a lightly-generalized version (one word up the abstraction ladder from the JD's phrase). Companion to the existing role-label paraphrase rule ([[feedback-paraphrase-generic-function-not-jd-scope]]): both surfaces carry identity, both must feel like the user's own claim rather than a mirror of the posting. Not the same as bullet-body scrubbing — bullet bodies keep vacancy-load-bearing specifics because bullets are PROOF; role labels and SUMMARY headlines carry IDENTITY.

  Worked example (Global M 2026-08-10, JD title `Senior Technical Product Manager – Platform & Data Ingestion`):
  - **Wrong:** `Senior Technical Product Manager for platform and data-ingestion products.` — mirrored the JD's "Platform & Data Ingestion" scope compound almost verbatim.
  - **Right:** `Senior Technical Product Manager for platform and data products.` — generalizes "data-ingestion" → "data" (one level up), keeps the platform + data identity that IS the user's genuine tenure focus.

  *Reason: the user flagged the Global M SUMMARY headline as JD-mirrored: "precise JD repetition is not trustful." The audience reads verbatim mirror as a CV engineered for this posting, which costs credibility on a senior screen — the same failure mode the role-label rule already prevents on role headers, now extended to the SUMMARY surface.*
- **SUMMARY closing clause must carry a recognized anchor, audience-relative.** The SUMMARY's closing clause is the recruiter's last read before deciding whether to scan the body. It must anchor on one of three signals; any single one suffices:
  1. **Universally recognized employer name** (Shell, BASF, FAANG-tier, named multinationals). Recognized regardless of vacancy domain.
  2. **Audience-recognized employer name**, checked against the vacancy's domain. Baker Hughes / Wintershall / Roxar / Emerson read on geo-data, energy, subsurface software vacancies. Tier-1 banks read on finance. Domain-specific names DROP on cross-domain screens. When unsure, treat as not recognized.
  3. **Self-contained proof** — a number, a scale phrase, a measurable outcome, or a recognized standard / methodology / technology stack (`WITSML`, `RAG`, `LLMOps`, `OCR-to-structured-schema`, `golden-set evals`). Works without any employer or product name. This is the safe default when no recognized name is available.

  **Product names** (`bevra.app`, `content-advisor`, internal product names) are valid anchors ONLY when paired with a proof predicate (measurable outcome, scale, audience-recognized customer/program). A bare product name carries the same problem as a bare unknown-company name. Naming an unknown product is not a fix for naming an unknown employer; it's the same failure.

  **Invalid constructs:** closing on `now delivering X at Rhodaris` / `currently building Y at <consultancy>` / `building <unknown product>` where the predicate adds no proof. The trailing slot evaporates because the audience has nothing to attach credit to.

  *Reason: the user flagged "now delivering AI-enabled delivery workflows at Rhodaris" on the Fugro CV (2026-06-26). First-pass fix anchored on `bevra.app` as a replacement, but the user clarified `bevra.app` is also low-recognition; recognition is audience-relative; Baker Hughes reads on oil & gas but not universally; Shell and BASF read more broadly. The three-anchor structure with the self-contained-proof escape hatch handles all cases without overfitting to one industry.*
